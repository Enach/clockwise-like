**One premise is wider than stated.** The ticket frames this as a gap in the generic list. It is really a **standing** gap. A domain that is on no list (a small ISP, a university alumni forwarder, a company's mailing-list alias) is open the same way to anyone whose address Google verified at it. Completing the list cannot close that. Only the account's standing can (§3.1).

## 1. Problem

Paceday groups accounts into an "organisation" by the domain of their email address. Members of an organisation can list every other member, with email, name and picture. They also share an organisation AI configuration, can try to become its SSO administrator, and see one another as "On Paceday" in Manager mode.

The grouping trusts the address's domain, not proof that the domain vouches for the person. Two consequences:

- **Consumer providers become fake organisations.** Anyone who signs in with an address at `orange.fr`, `web.de`, `hotmail.fr` or any of the many providers missing from the 21-entry list joins an organisation of every Paceday user at that provider, and can list them all. That is a bulk answer to "which of these people use Paceday", the same personal-data harm PAC-130 removed from Manager mode, for whole providers at once. The people listed get no notice and gave no consent.
- **Company organisations admit people the company never vouched for.** An address verified only by mailbox (a consumer Google account registered with a work address) joins the company's organisation. It sees the colleague list and the organisation AI configuration, can spend the organisation's AI key, and is bound by the organisation's "no personal keys" policy. PAC-71 already decided that such an address "resolves to nobody" at granting sites (`auth.yaml`, `CurrentUserResponse.addressStanding`). Organisation membership is a granting site that PAC-71 did not list.

The fix must keep the legitimate case: colleagues at a real company domain, vouched for by the company's identity provider, grouped together for SSO, organisation AI and the Manager badge.

### 2.1 How an organisation is created and joined

- **Created on first join.** `UpsertOrg` inserts the domain's organisation row, or renames it (`storage/orgs.go:19-28`). The name is derived from the domain (`domain.go:92-101`). Nothing else creates organisations. `CreateTeam` never sets `teams.org_id` (`storage/teams.go:52-57`), so that column is always null and nothing reads it.
- **Joined on every sign-in, whatever the standing.** The join rule reads only the domain. Every successful Google, Microsoft and SSO sign-in calls it, including a reach of an already-bound account (rule 1), for the account the identity is bound to and its stored address (`handlers_pac71_auth.go:186-189`). A failure is logged and reported, and the sign-in goes on (PAC-122 SF-071, `:198-204`).
- **Never left.** `SetUserOrg` only moves an account to the organisation it is told (`storage/orgs.go:53-79`). Nothing clears `users.org_id`, except deleting the organisation row (`ON DELETE SET NULL`, `migrations/007_organizations.up.sql:8`). A stored address never changes on sign-in (PAC-71 D-7), so membership, once made, is permanent.
- **Leaving ends organisation-AI consent.** When the organisation changes, all of the account's organisation-AI confirmations are deleted (PAC-87, `storage/orgs.go:41-52`, `:70-74`).
- **Which accounts carry which standing** (`storage/identity.go:67-72`; `storage/signin.go:289-300`):

| How the account was made or bound | Evidence | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| Google, address at the account's Google Workspace domain (`hd` equals the domain) | authoritative | domain_confirmed |
| Google, `gmail.com` / `googlemail.com` (`handlers_pac71_auth.go:83`) | authoritative | domain_confirmed (but these domains are on the consumer list) |
| Google, any other verified address (a consumer Google account on a non-Google address) | mailbox | unconfirmed |
| Microsoft, address on a domain verified in the user's Entra tenant (`:280-293`) | authoritative | domain_confirmed |
| Microsoft, otherwise | none | refused for a new account (`signin.go:146-148`) |
| Microsoft personal account (any `hotmail.*`, `outlook.*`, `live.*`) | n/a | refused until PAC-77 (`:261-266`) |
| SSO (OIDC) with a verified email at the provider's domain (`handlers_sso.go:168-187`) | authoritative | domain_confirmed |
| Legacy account (before PAC-71) not yet signed in since | none recorded | unconfirmed until its next sign-in binds it (migration 035) |

- **Who can make an SSO provider or an organisation AI configuration for a domain.** Only a member who passes the admin step-up:
  - **Google:** `hd` equals the organisation domain and the account is a Workspace admin (`api/handlers_sso_stepup.go:612-630`).
  - **Microsoft:** the tenant has verified the organisation domain and the account holds a qualifying Entra role (`:637-668`).
  - **Break-glass:** an operator-listed address held `domain_confirmed` (`:780-785`).

  So **no configuration can exist on a consumer-domain organisation**, except one an operator made through break-glass.

### 2.5 Why the obvious fixes do not work on their own

- **Completing the consumer list** cannot be done. Consumer mail domains number in the thousands, change over time, and include ISPs, regional portals and forwarders (§1). Any domain the list misses is open again. The list is also shared with sign-in detection (C-8), so every entry changes another feature.
- **MX-based provider detection** (PAC-97, `auth/sso_detect.go:316-390`) answers only "Google", "Microsoft" or "choose". It cannot tell a consumer provider from a company:
  - every host under `google.com` counts as Google (`:367`), so Gmail and a Google Workspace company look the same;
  - the Outlook.com consumer host is deliberately not matched (`:370-375`), so `hotmail.fr` is "choose", like any self-hosted company;
  - a provider that hosts its own mail (`orange.fr`, `web.de`) is indistinguishable from a company that does;
  - a failed lookup is "choose", cached for a minute (`:57-63`), so membership would depend on DNS weather.
- **Restricting the list to "org admins"**: Paceday has no persistent admin role. The step-up gives a single-use, purpose-bound grant that lasts minutes (`migrations/030_sso_admin_stepup.up.sql:17-33`). An admin role would be a new feature, and it would remove the colleague directory that PAC-130 rule (b) relies on.
- **The public suffix list** says which part of a name is registrable. It says nothing about who provides mail there, so it does not answer this question.

## 3. Desired behaviour

### 3.1 Membership requires the domain to vouch for the person [F, D-1]

An account belongs to the organisation of its address's domain **only when both hold**:
1. its standing is **domain-confirmed**: the identity provider that signed it in is the domain's own (Google Workspace for that domain, an Entra tenant that verified the domain, or the domain's SSO provider); and
2. the domain is **not on the consumer list**.

Every other account belongs to **no** organisation. This is PAC-71's granting-site rule applied to organisation membership: "only a `domain_confirmed` address acts as a person at the granting sites".

Why this rule:
- **It closes the oracle without depending on a complete list.** At a consumer provider nobody can be domain-confirmed. Gmail is the exception and is on the list, and Microsoft personal accounts are refused. So no consumer-domain organisation has members, and none can be listed.
- **It keeps every legitimate organisation feature.** SSO and organisation AI are writable only through a step-up that already requires the domain's own identity provider (§2.1). The members it removes are exactly the ones those features could never have been configured for.
- **Admin- or SSO-only membership was rejected** (D-2). It would make organisations exist only after an admin had stepped up. Most company accounts would lose the colleague badge (PAC-130 rule (b)) for no privacy gain, since a Workspace or Entra account at the domain was already vouched for by the company.

**Accepted residual.** "Domain-confirmed" trusts the identity provider's own verification of the domain: Google's for `hd`, Microsoft's for verified domains, the configured IdP for SSO. PAC-71 rests on the same trust, and this spec does not re-litigate it. A consumer mail provider that hosts its customers on Google Workspace or Entra would form a real organisation. That is the domain owner's choice, as for any company.

### 3.2 Membership follows the rule at every sign-in, and in both directions [F, D-3]

- At every successful sign-in, the account's membership is decided again from the rule: it joins its domain's organisation when it qualifies, and **leaves** any organisation when it does not.
- An account that does not qualify never causes an organisation to be **created**.
- Leaving follows PAC-87: every organisation-AI confirmation of the account ends, so a later rejoin asks again.
- A failed membership update keeps today's handling: the sign-in goes on, and the failure is logged and reported (SF-071).
- No organisation-membership answer anywhere (C-1 to C-7) may treat as a member an account that does not satisfy the rule at the moment of the read, **whatever stored it**: an older release, a fixture or an operator. How this is enforced is the contract's and the plan's choice.

### 3.3 The consumer list is extended, curated, not vendored [F, D-4]

The standing rule closes the oracle on its own. The list is still needed, because a consumer address **can** be domain-confirmed in three cases: Gmail today, Microsoft personal accounts once PAC-77 lands, and anything an operator creates through break-glass SSO. It is extended as defence in depth, with **curated** entries.

- **At least** these domains are added: `hotmail.fr`, `outlook.fr`, `live.fr`, `orange.fr`, `wanadoo.fr`, `free.fr`, `sfr.fr`, `laposte.net`, `web.de`, `gmx.de`, `gmx.net`, `gmx.fr`, `yahoo.de`, `t-online.de`.
- **Factory default for the rest:**
  - Microsoft's personal-account domains for the markets Paceday serves (the `hotmail.*`, `outlook.*`, `live.*` country variants), which PAC-77 needs anyway;
  - Yahoo and Apple country variants;
  - the major French, German and UK ISP mail domains.

  Each entry carries a one-line source in the list. The plan proposes the full set, and the challenger reviews it.
- **Vendoring a public free-mail list** (thousands of entries) was rejected (D-4):
  - those lists are compiled for sign-up filtering and mix in disposable and business domains;
  - here a false positive costs a real company its organisation, SSO routing and organisation AI;
  - a false negative costs nothing once the standing rule holds;
  - every entry also changes sign-in detection (C-8).
- **MX-based classification** was rejected for the reasons in §2.5. PAC-97's detection is not reused for membership.
- **Detection changes for the added domains**, from an MX-based answer to `generic`. PAC-98's per-entry table gains a row for each one (`auth/pac98_detect_test.go:66-89`). Microsoft's domains stay `generic` until PAC-77.
- **A future list change carries the same detach.** Accounts already in an organisation whose domain is newly listed leave it in the same release (§3.5), not only at their next sign-in (§3.2).

### 3.4 The member list: kept, narrowed by the rule, and honest about failures [F, D-7, D-8]

- The list returns the accounts of the caller's organisation under §3.1. It is the same set as PAC-130 rule (b), as today.
- A caller in no organisation (every consumer-domain account, every unconfirmed account) gets the empty list. The answer is the same whether zero or many accounts exist at the caller's domain (AC-1).
- A caller who cannot be read gets the 500, not the empty list (API-047, SF-075). A failed member read is already the 500 (`handlers_org.go:33-38`).
- The listed fields do not change. `provider` is documented as the three values written today.
- **Why keep it rather than remove it** (D-7):
  - PAC-130 rule (b) is justified by "the viewer can already read this in the member list". Removing the list would turn (b) into a per-address existence signal backed by nothing the viewer can see. Keeping (b) without the list would contradict PAC-130's principle, and dropping (b) would remove the badge for same-company colleagues.
  - After §3.1, the list is the directory of colleagues the company's own identity provider vouched for. That is the disclosure a company directory already makes.
  - **Removal stays the owner's call** (OQ-3). It costs nothing to consumers, because there are none, but it forces a PAC-130 amendment.

### 3.5 Existing memberships that fail the rule end at deploy [F, D-5, D-6]

A migration ends, at deploy, every membership that fails §3.1:
- every account whose standing is unconfirmed, **including unbound legacy accounts** (OQ-2). Such an account rejoins at its next sign-in if that sign-in confirms it;
- every account whose domain is on the extended consumer list.

For each such account, its organisation-AI confirmations end (PAC-87's leaving rule).

What the migration **keeps**:
- **Organisation rows** are not deleted, even when they are left with no members. Deleting a row would cascade to the operator record (`sso_events`), step-up grants, and organisation AI configuration and confirmations (`migrations/030:23`, `:42`; `031:18`, `:39`). A row with no members is unreachable: every reader reaches an organisation through a member.
- **Organisation AI configurations and SSO providers.** None can exist on a consumer-domain organisation except by break-glass (§2.1). On a company organisation they were written by a verified admin and stay valid for the remaining members.
- **Unused step-up grants** of detached accounts. They expire within minutes, and a non-member cannot consume one (C-5's gate step 1).

Effects that follow, intended and listed in the release note (§7):
- **A detached account that followed its organisation's AI** reads `nothing_configured` (row 6) until it chooses a key or rejoins. A detached account bound by "personal keys not allowed" is no longer bound.
- **PAC-130 rule (b) narrows** (§3.6).
- **G-3 residual.** An organisation row created by an account that is now detached keeps its `created_at`. A remaining member can still see when the first Paceday account at their own company's domain signed in. Accepted: it is their own company.

### 3.6 Effect on PAC-130's badge [F, D-9]

Rule (b) stays defined as "the accounts the member list returns to the viewer". After this spec:
- a viewer who is not domain-confirmed, or who is at a consumer domain, has an empty (b). For them only rule (a), shared teams, can make an address visible;
- an unconfirmed account at the viewer's own company domain is no longer in (b). Its address takes the estimate branch unless the two share a team.

What does **not** change:
- **No Paceday figures move.** Organisation-only accounts never got figures (PAC-60). ## 7. Rollback

- **Migration.** It only clears memberships and deletes organisation-AI confirmations (AC-7). The down migration is a no-op and **cannot restore** them. That is acceptable, because each cleared membership was the leak. A detached account that qualifies rejoins at its next sign-in, but must confirm organisation AI again (PAC-87). It runs under PAC-116's automatic stop-migrate-start (`docs/runbook.md:98-123`).
- **Image rollback.** The previous backend joins on the domain alone again, so squatters rejoin at their next sign-in and the oracle returns. That is expected on a rollback. Accounts that do not sign in stay detached, which is the safe direction.
- **Web-only rollback**: nothing to roll back (§3.7).
- **Release note:**
  - Accounts whose address is not confirmed by their domain's identity provider leave their organisation. This covers consumer Google accounts registered with a work address, and accounts not signed in since PAC-71. They lose the organisation AI (they see "nothing configured" until they choose a key or sign in again with their company account). They disappear from colleagues' member lists and from the Manager "On Paceday" badge unless they share a team.
  - Legacy accounts rejoin automatically at their next sign-in with their company account, and are asked to confirm the organisation AI again.
  - The erase is **irreversible**. An operator who wants full rollback fidelity snapshots the account-to-organisation association and `org_ai_confirmations` before deploy.
  - **Before deploy**, the release manager:
    - counts the memberships the migration will end, per organisation domain (the owner sees the number: OQ-2);
    - checks that no SSO provider and no organisation AI configuration exists for a newly listed consumer domain. One that exists was made through break-glass and needs an owner decision before release; it is not erased silently.
  - Sign-in detection answers "pick a provider" (`generic`) for the newly listed consumer domains.

## Challenge — 2026-11-19

**Verdict**: REVISE (reject in the role file's terms). The direction holds: D-1 closes the ticket's oracle without needing a complete list, and D-7, D-8 and D-9 are sound. Four blocking items must be fixed in the body before contract work. Read against clockwise-like `ddd2cd2` (spec head `b81e912`), smart-calendar-flow `5920bd6` and the Linear issue (no comments).

### Blocking

1. **Standing never changes after binding, so the promised remedy for mailbox-bound accounts does not exist.**
   - `reachBound` never rewrites `address_standing` ("The stored address and standing are never changed", `storage/signin.go:175-179`). Rule 3 refuses any new identity for an address that a bound account already carries (`storage/signin.go:125-128`).
   - So U, a consumer Google account bound on mailbox evidence to `u@acme.com`, can never become domain-confirmed. When U later signs in with the real Workspace account for the same address, that sign-in is **refused**. It is not a rejoin.
   - The only route to confirmation is the operator step `ReassignAccount` with authoritative evidence (`storage/operator.go:184-260`).
   - Three statements in the body are therefore false for every non-legacy account they cover:
     - §7's release note: "until they … sign in again with their company account".
     - §3.5's "rejoins at its next sign-in if that sign-in confirms it".
     - D-3's framing that membership "is decided again" at sign-in. Standing is fixed, so a re-decision can only flip on a consumer-list change or an operator step.
   - OQ-1 also misses a real owner consequence. When a company moves to Workspace or Entra **after** staff signed up with consumer Google accounts on their work addresses, those accounts stay outside the organisation for good, and they cannot even sign in with the new company identity, unless an operator reassigns them.
   - **Resolves it:**
     - §3.2, §3.5 and §7 state that standing is permanent for a bound account and name the recourse, which is the operator reassign, or open an OQ for a self-service path.
     - The release note says that support must handle these accounts.
     - OQ-1 includes the "company adopts Workspace later" case.
     - An AC pins it: U, after detach, signs in with the Workspace identity for the same address. The expected answer is the refusal, not a rejoin.

2. **§3.2's "whatever stored it" has no AC for the ticket's own oracle or for G-4's actual harm, which is spending the key.**
   - AC-4's planted variant repeats only these checks: the org-AI read, confirm and patch; the SSO list, options and create; the settings read; the key choice; and rule (b). It does not plant:
     - **G-1.** U's own member list must be `[]`, and A1's list must exclude U.
     - **G-3.** `/api/auth/me` must have no `org`.
     - **G-4 spend.** Plant U's association **and** an `org_ai_confirmations` row, then check that an AI call does not use the organisation configuration. The AI call paths are `nlp.Parse` (`nlp/parser.go:170`, which MCP's `clockwise_parse_command` also reaches), meeting briefs (`api/handlers_meeting_briefs.go:91`) and the probe (`api/handlers_llm.go:57`). All of them read raw `users.org_id` through `ReadOrgAIState` (`storage/org_ai.go:172-197`).
     - `deleteSsoProvider` and the step-up callback. The callback compares the state's `OrgID` with raw `user.OrgID` (`api/handlers_sso_stepup.go:458`).
   - The body also never says whether the read-time rule includes "the organisation **of its address's domain**", meaning `organizations.domain` equals the address's domain, or only standing plus the list. Production can store a mismatch:
     - `FreeAddress` rewrites the address to `freed+<id>@paceday.invalid` and keeps `org_id` (`storage/operator.go:135-139`).
     - The e2e fixtures plant accounts in organisations of another domain (item 3).
   - **Resolves it:**
     - Extend AC-4's planted variant to G-1, G-3, the three AI-spend paths (with a planted confirmation), `deleteSsoProvider` and the callback.
     - State in §3.1 whether the domain match is part of the read-time rule. If it is, AC-4 also plants a domain-confirmed account into another domain's organisation.

3. **The protection table and the fixture inventory are wrong for e2e and incomplete for Go.**
   - §2.4 says the `llm-bring-your-own` journey "stays valid if its user is one of" the seeded `domain_confirmed` users. It is not one of them:
     - Its user comes from a local `freshUser` that inserts with the default standing, `unconfirmed` (`e2e/tests/llm-bring-your-own.spec.ts:22-26`).
     - The test puts that user at `@paceday.test` into an organisation for a **different** domain, `byok-….paceday.test` (`:97-100`).
   - Two more e2e journeys plant unconfirmed members by SQL, and the spec does not mention them:
     - `e2e/tests/llm-org-ai.spec.ts:31-41` (admin, member and other);
     - `e2e/tests/sso-stepup.spec.ts:29-45`.
   - §6 cites `llm-bring-your-own.spec.ts:92-140` as a protection, yet under §3.2 it fails, and so do the other two.
   - §6 lists 7 Go fixture sites. There are at least 13 more (see "Consumers this breaks").
   - The stated risk, "a 403 where a gate was expected", applies to each of them.
   - **Resolves it:** §6 names every site below and states that each gets a domain-confirmed account at a domain matching its organisation. The e2e rows of the protection table say they are amended, not that they "stay valid".

4. **The irreversible migration has no concrete gate, and its pre-check contradicts D-5.**
   - §7 says that an SSO provider or organisation-AI configuration on a newly listed consumer domain "needs an owner decision before release; it is not erased silently".
   - D-5 still detaches every member of that domain unconditionally. Nothing says whether the migration aborts, skips that domain or proceeds.
   - The release manager's two checks are prose, not queries, and neither has a stop condition.
   - The migration's consumer list must be a SQL literal, while the list itself lives in Go (`backend/domain/domain.go:8-17`). No AC ties the two together. AC-7 exercises a single domain (`hotmail.fr`).
   - **Resolves it:**
     - State the migration's behaviour when such a configuration exists: abort, skip, or proceed and record it.
     - Give the exact pre-deploy queries: the count per organisation domain of memberships ended, and the configurations present on listed domains. State the stop condition.
     - Add to AC-7: the migration's domain set equals `domain.GenericDomains()` of the same release, enumerated, so every listed domain is detached.
     - Apply the same requirement to D-10's future migrations.

### Non-blocking

1. **D-4: D-1 makes the list nearly irrelevant to membership, and the spec should say so plainly.**
   - Under D-1, nobody at `orange.fr`, `free.fr`, `sfr.fr`, `wanadoo.fr`, `laposte.net`, `web.de`, `gmx.*` or `t-online.de` can be domain-confirmed, except through an operator reassign or break-glass SSO. Adding those domains changes no membership. Their only effect is the detection change (C-8).
   - The spec's own rationale (§3.3: Gmail, PAC-77's Microsoft personal domains, operator paths) justifies the `hotmail.*`, `outlook.*` and `live.*` variants. It does not justify the ISPs.
   - The false-negative risk for "open" providers that can never all be listed is therefore zero under D-1, and list completeness is not a security property.
   - Recommended changes:
     - Say so.
