## 1. Problem

A team owner uses a Paceday team for three things:
- finding a time that suits everyone;
- seeing the team's availability;
- protecting no-meeting hours.

Today the only way to put someone on a team is an invitation. The person must accept it while signed in to a Paceday account that holds that exact address, confirmed (`handlers_teams.go:316-324`). Until they accept:
- they are not on the team;
- they are absent from Find a time;
- the owner cannot even see them in the member list.

Most people an owner coordinates with will never sign up, or have not yet. The owner already sees their busy times through their own calendar. The owner wants to add them by email and be done.

The current flow also misleads in places this work touches:
- the invite dialog promises a Pending entry that never appears (SF-051);
- Find a time claims "N of N free" for every slot (SF-054);
- a colleague whose calendar cannot be read is shown as free all day (SF-032, fixed by the prerequisite).

Owner, 2026-11-18: adding someone to a team should not require them to accept. The owner should be able to add them without inviting them to use Paceday. Inviting them stays a separate, explicit action.

## 3. Desired behaviour

### 3.1 Adding a person

On the team's member management, an owner adds a person by email, with an optional display name.
- **Suggestions** come from the owner's calendar contacts. They exclude every team member of both kinds and every address with a pending invitation.
- **Required wording** [O]. The form states, before submitting:
  - that the owner is responsible for having a reason to add this person;
  - that no email is sent to them;
  - that every member of the team will see their name and email;
  - that their free/busy will be read through **the owner's** calendar connection whenever any member uses Find a time.
- **Immediate effect**: the person is on the team at once. Nothing is sent and nothing is asked of them. The owner is told the person was "added", never "sent" or "invited".
- **No existence oracle**: the answer, and the member list, are the same whether or not the address belongs to a Paceday account.
- **Repeats**: adding the same address again, in any case or with surrounding spaces, changes nothing and creates no second entry.
- **Refusals**: adding the owner's own address, or the address of a Paceday member of the team, is refused with a reason. Nothing changes. An account carries one address (§2.1), so comparing against each member's address reveals no link between addresses.

### 3.2 Two kinds of member

Every member is one of two kinds:
- a **Paceday member**, who joined by accepting an invitation;
- an **added person**, labelled **"Added (no Paceday access)"** [F, M-8].

The kind depends only on whether the person accepted an invitation. It never depends on whether an account exists for the address. Every member sees an added person's name, email and label, as they see Paceday members today [O].

**Backward compatibility.** A client built before this change sees exactly the Paceday members it saw before, in the same shape. Added people reach only clients that understand them. This holds:
- during every release window (README §1 order: the backend ships before the web);
- on a web-only rollback.

### 3.3 What an added person gets and gives

Nothing:
- They are not told.
- They cannot see the team, even if they have a Paceday account.
- Their Paceday data is never read for this team: no analytics, focus blocks, habits or settings.

Their availability comes **only** from free/busy read through the credential of the owner who added them (OQ-1). If that read cannot see their calendar (the prerequisite makes provider errors "unknown"), or their address is at a personal-mail domain, their availability is **unknown**. It is never free.

### 3.4 The owner's credential serves every member [O]

Any member may run Find a time over a team with added people. The read of added people's free/busy then uses **the owner's** calendar credential, whoever asked. The owner chose this knowingly (2026-11-18). Its consequences, all to be stated in the contract and pinned by AC-29:

- **Quota.** Every member's Find a time spends the owner's provider quota (Google per-user quota; Microsoft throttling).
- **Audit trail.** The provider attributes these reads to the owner's account and Paceday's OAuth client (Google Workspace and Microsoft 365 audit logs). Paceday's own activity log does not record each read [F].
- **Credential loss.** If the owner's refresh is refused during **any** member's request, it is recorded as the **owner's** credential loss, exactly as if the owner had asked. The requesting member's own credential is never touched.
- **Inference.** A member learns, at 15-minute granularity inside 09:00–18:00 UTC on the day asked:
  - the union of the busy times of everyone counted;
  - for each offered slot, how many members are free and how many are unknown.

  With one added person, a slot missing from the answer can reveal that this person is busy then. No per-person busy interval is ever returned to anyone.

### 3.5 Find a time and availability

- **Who is searched**: Paceday members (as today) and added people.
- **Blocking**: a busy added person blocks a slot exactly as a busy Paceday member does. Following PAC-133 D-11 (contract stage, challenge M-4): busy windows the provider reported for an address block even when that address is unknown; an added person who is unknown with no reported windows does not block.
- **Counts**: each offered slot states how many members are **free** and how many are **unknown**, out of the total. "Busy" is not reported, because an offered slot never has a busy member (§2.4). A Paceday member whose calendar could not be read counts as **unknown** [F, M-2]. Whether that member blocks slots stays as today (they do not), so the pinned behaviour holds.
- **Zones**: no-meeting zones apply as today.
- **Scheduling from a slot**: everyone on the team except the user is put in the attendee list, added people included. The attendee addresses travel inside the app, **not in the page address** [F, minor 11]. Nothing on the server side carries them, so no contract change is needed for this.
- **Batching** [F, M-4]: added people's free/busy is requested in batches within the provider's documented limit: 50 per Google request, 20 per Microsoft request (§2.5).
  - Each batch has its own time limit.
  - A failed batch makes only its own addresses unknown.
  - No one is reported unknown because of how many people are on the team.
  - **Batches run concurrently**, at most **3 at a time** [F, m2-3], inside the request budget. At the cap, the worst case is 3 Microsoft batches, which run in a single round. A slow tenant therefore costs one batch time limit, not three in sequence.
  - A batch that the request budget prevents from starting, or cuts short, answers the PAC-81 budget error (the 503), as for a member read. A batch that fails on its own time limit makes its addresses unknown.

### 3.6 Analytics

- **An added person's row** is availability-only: meeting time from free/busy, with no focus figures and no focus score. It is labelled so.
- **Persistence**: no figure for an added person is stored beyond the existing 15-minute free/busy cache:
  - on the team path, ever;
  - on the Manager path, for a team-added person with no roster entry.

  A roster entry the manager created explicitly keeps today's stored figures (§3.9).
- **Visibility** [F, M-1, D-6]:
  - owners see added people's figures;
  - non-owners see the row (name, email, label) with **no figures**;
  - added people are excluded from team averages.
- **Correctness** (SF-053, SF-041):
  - every row is attributed to its own member, even when two members share a name;
  - a member with no measured week shows "no data", never zero;
  - no row has invented free time;
  - the week shown is the week asked for.

### 3.7 Inviting to Paceday

**"Pending" defined** [F, M2-3]. Throughout this spec, an invitation is **pending** only when it is not answered **and** its expiry is still in the future, judged by the database's clock: stored status pending and expiry after now.
- An invitation whose expiry has passed is **lapsed**, whatever its stored status says. The stored status is only swept on preview and accept (§2.2), so an unopened invitation can still read "pending" long after it lapsed.
- This definition applies to every use of "pending" in this spec: the de-duplication, the one-per-address rule, the "already pending" answer, the migration, removal, the cap and the web.

Inviting stays a separate, explicit action. It is offered on an added person ("Invite to Paceday") and still accepts an address not yet on the team.
- **New address**: inviting an address not on the team also adds the person, within the cap (OQ-4). The entry shows the invitation as pending.
- **Already pending**: if an invitation for the address is pending on the team, the answer is **success-shaped**. It reports the existing invitation, creates no second one and sends no second email (SF-012).
- **Lapsed**: if the only invitations for the address on the team are lapsed, a fresh invitation is created and its email is sent, exactly as a first invitation. In the same step, any lapsed row still stored as pending is marked expired, so at most one invitation per team and address is ever pending.
- **Already a Paceday member** [F, m2-4]: inviting the address of a Paceday member of the team is answered as success, says the person is already a member, and creates no invitation and sends no email.
- **Name**: the display name typed with an invitation is kept (SF-051).
- **Acceptance**: the person accepts while signed in to an account holding the address confirmed, as today.
  - **If they have an added entry**, it becomes a Paceday member: same entry, one membership, in one step. This never changes the member count, so it is allowed even at or over the cap.
  - **If they have no entry** (a legacy invitation from before this change), the membership is created in one step if the team is below the cap. If the team is at or over the cap, acceptance is refused with a clear text (the team is full), and nothing changes.
  - **After acceptance** their availability is read with their own credential.

### 3.8 Removing

- **Who**: an owner can remove any member of either kind. A Paceday member can still remove themselves. The last owner can never be removed.
- **Effect**: removing an added person deletes their address from the team's data:
  - their entry;
  - every invitation for that address on that team, pending or not;
  - any stored figure for them.

  No old link can bring them back.
- **The person is named by address within the team.** If an acceptance upgraded them before the removal ran, the removal removes the membership that resulted. Removing a person the owner asked to remove never leaves them a member [F, M2-1].
- **Removal and acceptance in either order end the same way: the person is absent, with no invitation left.** What the accepter is told depends on the order:
  - **removal first**: the invitation is deleted, so the acceptance is answered "invite not found" (404) and writes nothing;
  - **acceptance first**: the acceptance succeeds (204) and the person is briefly a Paceday member; the removal then deletes that membership and the team's invitations for the address.
- **Both paths** [F, B-2]: the Manager-mode team-scoped removal does exactly this for the team membership, under the same ordering as the team removal (D-8: the team lock first, then the owner check and every read the decision rests on, inside the same transaction).
- **Team membership and the manager's roster are separate** [F, M2-2]:
  - Removing someone from the team, by either path, does not delete the manager's roster entry for that address: neither the global roster row nor its 1:1 history.
  - The Manager-mode team-scoped removal still drops that team's assignment, as it does today (`storage/manager.go:201-208`, §2.3). That is its existing roster effect, not a team effect.
  - In reverse, deleting a roster entry without a team (the Manager global delete) never touches team membership.
- **Web**: it says "removed" only when the server removed someone (SF-089).

### 3.9 Manager mode [F, B-2, M2-2, M2-4]

**Two concepts, kept apart.** A person **added to a team** (PAC-129) and an entry in **a manager's private roster** are different things. Revision 2's removal of Manager-mode features is reverted.

- **The shipped Manager features stay unchanged.** The Manager Team tab keeps its "Add person" dialog, with its cadence field, contact suggestions and today's behaviour, protected by `TeamAddPerson.test.tsx` (§6). Detection and confirmation of 1:1s stay unchanged too.
- **Two add controls, each saying what it does** [M-8]:
  - The team's member management offers the team add of §3.1, worded as adding to the team without Paceday access.
  - The Manager tab's "Add person" keeps its wording about tracking 1:1s.
  - Neither control's wording describes the other's effect.
- **Explicit manager actions create roster entries.** If the manager adds an address through Manager "Add person", or confirms it in detection, that creates a roster entry with today's behaviour, even when the address is also an added person on the team:
  - cadence editing;
  - 1:1 scheduling;
  - figures stored in the Manager analytics cache.

  Its existing account-existence signal is tracked separately in **PAC-130** and is not changed here.
- **A team-added person with no roster entry** is an added person of the team for whom the manager has **no** roster entry assigned to that team. In the manager's team-scoped roster this person appears **read-only**:
  - **no account signal**: no "On Paceday" mark and no Paceday-user flag;
  - **no cadence editing and no 1:1 scheduling**: both are refused, and nothing is written. In particular, the code path that writes a roster row on first patch or schedule must not run for them (`handlers_manager.go:710-746`, §2.3);
  - **patch and schedule never write** a roster row or an assignment for them [m3-1]. Detection's background scan keeps writing its global, team-less `auto` roster rows as today (`engine/manager.go` `DetectTeam` → `UpsertManagerTeamMemberCtx`); such a row is not assigned to the team, so it does not change the person's status under this definition;
  - **no Manager figure** is stored for them by any read.
- **Pre-existing state stays.** Assignments, roster rows and cached Manager figures that existed before a person was added to the team are the manager's own data. They are left as they are, and that person is not a "team-added person with no roster entry".

### 3.10 Limits [F, M-10]

- **Cap**: a team holds at most **50 members, owner included**, both kinds together. Invitations do not count toward the cap; members do. An add, an invitation-that-adds, or a legacy acceptance that would exceed it is refused with the limit stated. Concurrent writes never take a team past it.
- **Teams already above 50 at deploy** keep their members. They cannot add, invite a new address, or accept a legacy invitation until they are below the cap. They can always remove.

### 3.11 Privacy and erasure [O]

- **Lawful basis**: the add is silent, and the owner is responsible, on legitimate interest (as with an address book). No email is sent. Every member sees the person's email.
- **Removal** deletes the address from the team's data (§3.8). A manager's roster entry for the same address is that manager's own data. Team removal does not delete it (§3.8, §3.9). The runbook procedure below does.
- **Runbook**: the runbook gains an operator procedure that erases one address from **all** team and Manager data. It covers:
  - added entries;
  - invitations of every status;
  - Manager roster rows and assignments;
  - 1:1 occurrences;
  - cached Manager figures.

  The procedure states that a person **with** an account must be searched by address too. Their added entries are not linked to their account, so deleting the account does not reach them.
- **Follow-up**: a cross-cutting "erase address" tool is filed separately as **PAC-134** (§5).

### 3.12 Demo mode

A demo session can add, list, invite, remove and run Find a time with added people, with no network call.
- An invited person in the demo is an added person with a pending invitation.
- Added people count in demo Find a time and in its attendee list.
- The demo never guesses an account from the domain.

### 3.13 Session and account deletion

- **Re-check**: every new write runs under the PAC-71 in-request re-check. The writes are: the add, the removal, the invitation-that-adds, the upgrade on acceptance, the legacy-acceptance create, and the Manager-path removal. A stale session writes nothing.
- **Account references**: any new reference from an added entry to an account (for example, who added the person) is classified in the PAC-71 emptiness inventory.
- **Deleting the adding owner's account**:
  - if that owner is the creator, the team, and its added people, go with it, as today;
  - otherwise, the added people stay, and their availability is unknown until another owner re-adds them (OQ-1).

## 7. Rollback

- **Migration.** It is additive, apart from the invitation dedupe of AC-26, and is applied under PAC-116's stop-traffic rule (`docs/runbook.md:98-130`).
- **Images.** An image-only rollback is refused (`migrate_when_stopped.go:449-450`). Either fix forward, or restore the pre-deploy backup and start the previous image.
- **The down migration loses data**:
  - every added person;
  - the older duplicate pending invitations removed by the dedupe, which no down step can bring back. Their tokens stop working at the up migration, not at rollback; the newest invitation for each address keeps working.
  - Paceday memberships, including upgraded ones, survive.
- **Release note for a rollback:** owners re-add the people they had added.
- **Web-only rollback** is safe by requirement: the old web sees only Paceday members (§3.2, AC-8). It cannot show or remove added people, so it should still be avoided.
